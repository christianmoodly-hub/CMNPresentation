How the controls work together

Defence in depth (layering + diversity): no single failure exposes research data.

Prioritised action plan

Immediate (0-72 hours): Contain

  • Reset phished credentials, revoke sessions, enforce MFA on admin/registration
  • Quarantine the laptop; block the outbound leak path; preserve logs
  • Check DNS records and flush caches

Short-term (1-3 months): Prevent and detect

  • Deploy mail gateway, NAC, DNS hardening
  • Deploy IPS/IDS and SIEM with tuned alerts
  • Run phishing awareness training

Longer-term (3-12 months): Mature

  • Re-segment network, with a dedicated research VLAN
  • Full DLP rollout after data classification
  • Incident response plan, tabletop exercises, align to NIST CSF / ISO 27001

Why this order, and what it costs us

Contain before prevent, prevent before mature: an active incident must be stopped first, then the entry points closed, then structure improved.
Low cost, high impact first: MFA and quarantine are cheap and cut the most risk quickly.
Dependencies: SIEM needs log sources deployed first; DLP needs classified data first.
Trade-off: NAC and MFA add friction at registration peaks. Managed by phased rollout, off-peak enforcement, a helpdesk surge team and clear user communication.

Key takeaways

01

One phished password can start a chain of incidents; people are the first target.

02

No single control is enough; layered, diverse controls contain failure.

03

Act in order: contain now, prevent next, mature over time.
